Cities Are Technology

The most powerful technology humans ever built is the city. We have stopped treating it that way.

We talk about technology as if it lives on our phones. But the most powerful technology humans ever built is the city. It decides how far you walk, who you meet by accident, how long you spend in a car and how much of your day belongs to you.

For most of history, cities were designed on purpose. Someone chose where the plaza went, how wide the streets were, which way the buildings faced. Then, somewhere in the last century, we handed that job to traffic engineers and zoning codes, and the city became a by-product instead of a product.

Design is a decision

Every street is an argument about what matters. A six-lane road says the car matters more than the child crossing it. A plaza says that strangers should meet. When nobody makes those arguments on purpose, the default wins, and the default is rarely human.

This is why I work on Northpoint Cities. The first idea is Anthropos City, on the coast of Azuero: a round plaza at the center, eight avenues leaving it like the points of a star, rings that grow by the golden ratio, and everything a family needs within a fifteen-minute walk. Anthropos means human being. The name is the brief.

Building again

It is fashionable to say that we cannot build anymore. I think the truth is simpler: we stopped trying. The tools have never been better. We can model a city before we pour a foundation, simulate its traffic, its light and its heat, and measure whether people are actually happier inside it.

We build our cities, and then our cities build us.

If that is true, then choosing how to build is one of the most important decisions a civilization makes. I would like ours to start making it on purpose again.
