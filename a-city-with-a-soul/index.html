A City With a Soul

My vision for smart cities: let machines do the work, and give people back what made us human. Beauty, passion, glory, purpose.

This is not a prediction. Nobody gets to decide what the future will be. It is my vision: the future I would like to help build, and the reasons I think it is worth building.

We live in a world made entirely by other people. Every street, every screen, every schedule was designed by someone. And looking at what we designed, it is hard not to feel that something went wrong. Wars that never seem to end. Record rates of depression and loneliness. Algorithms built to keep us scrolling instead of living. Cities organized around the commute and the clock.

Life was not supposed to feel like this.

What the factory took

For most of history, a free life was lived in public. A Roman citizen spent the morning at work and the afternoon in the forum, the baths, the gymnasium and the theater. Life was organized around family, faith, the city and the seasons. Work was part of a day, not the whole of a life.

Then the factory arrived. We left the fields and the forums for the mill. Time began to belong to the clock. Families scattered to follow wages, and a person slowly became what they did for a living. Two hundred years later, we still live inside that arrangement, only now the mill is a screen.

Smart cities, for once, for humans

When people say smart city, they usually mean sensors and dashboards. I mean something else: a city intelligent enough to work so its people do not have to. One that makes its own energy from the sun, its own water from the sea, its own food from autonomous farms, and runs its services with intelligent systems.

Superintelligence and abundant energy can finally end the trade the factory forced on us. The question is what we do with the time we get back. My answer is the one Rome already gave: the forum, the bath, the theater, the stadium, the academy.

Beauty, passion, glory

I want cities that are beautiful on purpose. Stone, water, shade and light. Plazas where strangers meet. Places that make you want to train, to create, to argue, to fall in love, to build something that outlasts you. A life measured not by productivity but by excellence, friendship, beauty and the common good.

That is what Anthropos Cities is about. Anthropos means human being. The first city, on the Pacific coast, is planned around a rotunda, avenues that open to the sea and a life lived in public again.

Not leisure. Purpose.

I may be wrong about how it happens. I am not wrong that it is worth trying. We built the world we live in. We can build a more human one.
