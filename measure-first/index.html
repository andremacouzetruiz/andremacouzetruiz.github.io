Measure First, Believe Second

What goes unmeasured gets told however we like. An uncomfortable number beats a beautiful story.

I once built a backtest to prove that my trading strategy worked. It proved the opposite. Thirteen years of one-minute data, real costs, out-of-sample tests, and the answer was clear: no edge.

That afternoon taught me more than any winning month. The strategy was not the problem. The problem was that I believed in it before I had measured it.

Stories are cheap

Humans are storytelling machines. We see a pattern three times and call it a law. We remember the trades that worked and forget the ones that did not. Without a number in front of us, we will always tell the version of events that flatters us.

Measurement is not cold. It is a form of respect for reality. It says: I care more about what is true than about what I want to be true.

The habit

Now I try to apply it everywhere. Before I build a product, I decide what number would tell me it failed. Before I change a rule, I write down what I expect to happen. When the result comes back uncomfortable, I keep it.

An uncomfortable number beats a beautiful story.

Most of the progress I have made came from those moments when the number disagreed with me, and I listened.
